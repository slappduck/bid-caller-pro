# Selling CurbCall Pro

What to do, in order, to get the first paying customers. Written to be used
between jobs: each part is a few minutes. Email outreach has its own rules in
`OUTREACH.md`; read that before sending any email.

The goal for the first 30 days: **10 paying customers.** Not more features.

---

## 1. Before you sell (one sitting, about 30 minutes)

1. **Merge the open pull request.** Until it is live, trial users don't get the
   automatic document reading, which is the best part of the product.
2. **Sign up as a stranger.** On your phone, with an email you've never used
   on CurbCall: sign up, scan your area, open a bid marked "items ready", and
   compare its line items to the real bid form. If anything is wrong, it is
   wrong for every new customer. Tell Claude what you saw.
3. **Buy it once.** Subscribe monthly with your own card, check the app
   unlocks within a minute or two, then refund yourself in Stripe. A broken
   checkout loses every sale silently.
4. **Decide the trial length.** Seven days is short for bid work: a contractor
   may not have a bid due that week and never sees what the app does. 14 days
   fits better. (Claude can change it; it's one number.)

## 2. The 60-second demo

This is the whole pitch, and it works on their phone or yours:

1. Open **curbcallpro.com**, type their ZIP in the box at the top, tap
   **See bids near me**. They see the open sidewalk, curb and ramp bids near
   them, with towns and due dates. That is the moment that sells it: work
   they didn't know about.
2. Tap **Start free**, sign up, scan. Open a bid marked **items ready**: the
   bid form's line items are already filled in, with what the same work went
   for and a target price.
3. Show the bid form filled in, and the addendum alert.

Say: "That took a minute. How long does it take you to find and price one of
those now?"

## 3. Your first 10 customers: local, by phone or in person

**Who:** concrete and flatwork contractors within an hour of you (Aurora,
Monett, Springfield, Joplin, Branson, Neosho, Carthage). Find them on Google
Maps ("concrete contractor"), at the supply house, and on trucks at job sites.
Smaller outfits (2 to 15 people) are the best fit: they bid public work but
have nobody whose job is finding it.

**Calling:**

> Hi, this is Josh in Aurora. I built an app that finds the city and county
> sidewalk, curb and ramp bids around here and gets them priced. Right now
> there are [N] open within 50 miles of you, [one by name, e.g. "Republic's
> sidewalk extension, due the 20th"]. Do you bid that kind of work?
>
> (yes) Can I text you the link? It's free for a week, no card. If you want,
> I'll stop by and show you on your phone in five minutes.

Look up their area on the landing page before you call, so you can name a
real bid near them. That one detail is what makes it a call they listen to.

**When they say:**

- *"I already get bids from [BidNet / Dodge / the city's email list]."*
  "This covers the small towns and counties those miss, and it reads the bid
  form and prices it for you. Run it next to what you use for a week and see
  what it finds that they don't."
- *"$49 a month is a lot."* "One job pays for years of it. If it doesn't find
  you a bid worth bidding in the trial, don't pay."
- *"I don't do public work."* "Most of these are small: a few blocks of
  sidewalk, a handful of ramps. Same work you do for anyone, and the city pays
  on time." (If still no, thank them and move on.)
- *"Send me something."* Text the landing page link and their ZIP: "Type your
  ZIP at the top, it shows what's open near you."

**Close it on the spot:** help them sign up on their own phone while you're
talking. A trial started later mostly doesn't start.

**Ask every customer for:** a review in the app (Account tab) and one
contractor they know. Referral links are in the Account tab: when the friend
subscribes, they get two extra weeks and the customer gets a free month.

## 4. Ready-mix plants as a sales channel

Plants make money when their customers win public work, and CurbCall sends
plants quote requests for every bid it prices. A plant salesperson who tells
contractors about CurbCall is a free sales rep.

**The pitch to a plant manager or salesperson:**

> I built an app that finds the city and county concrete bids around here
> and sends the contractor a ready-mix quote request to the plants near the
> job. Your customers win more of that work, you sell more yards. Would you
> mention it to your contractors? I'll give you a link that gets them two
> extra weeks free when they subscribe.

**How:** make an account for the plant (any email they give you), open
Account, and give them the referral link. Contractors who subscribe through
it get two extra weeks. Ask the plant if you can leave cards or a flyer at
the counter.

## 5. Facebook groups and local associations

Concrete contractor groups (national and Missouri/Arkansas/Kansas/Oklahoma),
and local builder or contractor associations. Post something useful, weekly,
not an ad:

> Open sidewalk, curb & ADA ramp bids in SW Missouri this week:
> - 2026 Sidewalk Replacement, Springfield, due Nov 18
> - ADA Curb Ramp Improvements Phase 3, Nixa, due Nov 20
> - South St Curb & Gutter, Ozark, due Dec 2
> (more at curbcallpro.com, type your ZIP at the top)

Get the list from the landing page's ZIP box, or sign yourself up for the
free Monday bid email for your ZIP (bottom of the landing page) and paste it.
Read each group's rules first; some only allow promotion on certain days.

## 6. Email outreach, when you restart it

Rules are in `OUTREACH.md` (five a day, researched, hand-written, working
opt-out). What changes: **lead with the bids, not the agency count.** A real
bid near them is useful even if they never sign up, and that is what gets
replies. Look up their town on the landing page first.

> Subject: 3 open sidewalk bids near Joplin
>
> Hi Mike,
>
> I saw [the personal line from the prospect list]. These are open right now
> within 50 miles of you:
>
> - Main St Sidewalk Replacement, Carthage, due Nov 19
> - ADA Ramps 2026, Webb City, due Nov 24
> - Curb & Gutter Repairs, Neosho, due Dec 3
>
> I built CurbCall Pro to find these and price them: it reads each bid form
> and gives a target price from what the same work went for. Your area is at
> curbcallpro.com/go/[slug], free for a week, no card.
>
> Joshua Hukel
> CurbCall Pro, Oblique Systems, LLC

Never call the agency count "bids", "jobs" or "projects". No em dashes.

## 7. The numbers that matter

The Monday business email already reports them. Watch four:

| Number | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Site visits | Is anyone arriving? (If not: more calls, posts, emails) |
| Trials started | Does the landing page convince? (Visits to trials) |
| Trials that read a bid | Do they reach the good part? (If not: the app's first minutes) |
| Paid | Does the product earn it? (Trials to paid) |

Fix the first number that's weak, in that order. Most early products fail at
the first one: nobody arrives.

## 8. Pricing levers worth considering

- **Founding-customer price.** "The first 20 contractors lock in $29 a month
  for as long as they stay." A reason to decide now, and a thank-you to the
  people who take a chance on a new product. (Needs a Stripe price; Claude
  can wire the app side.)
- **14-day trial**, as above.
- **Annual at $399** is already about 4 months free. Mention it to anyone who
  wins a job in their first month.
